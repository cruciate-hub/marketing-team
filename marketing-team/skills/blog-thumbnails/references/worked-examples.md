# Worked examples

The style is fixed; the concept is the variable. A good concept makes the post's argument readable in one second, at 502px wide, without the title.

How to go from a post to concepts is in social-plus-illustration (`ideation.md`, `composition-library.md`). These are the posts done so far and what each taught.

## Worked example: "Push Notifications Have Hit a Ceiling. Social Hasn't"

Three concepts were built; C was chosen.

- **A — Breakthrough card.** Grey notification slabs compress against a dashed "ceiling"; a big blue-gradient social card breaks through, replies rising out of frame. Verdict: liked, but the full blue card is too much colour, and too illustrative.
- **B — Breakout chart.** A huge dashboard panel; grey area plateaus under the ceiling, a blue line with avatars exits the top of the panel. Verdict: liked; very literal, less distinctive (charts are familiar).
- **C — Post over a wall of pushes (chosen).** Background: rows of push pills, mostly bars, four with real low-contrast copy ("We miss you", "Your cart is waiting", "Don't miss out", "Last chance"). Front: a large grey social post cropped off right and bottom, with a readable name (a fictional placeholder; use American names going forward) and one blue "Reply" button. No ceiling line needed — the wall itself says "saturated".

What the iterations taught (style):
1. First drafts were too detailed → fewer, bigger shapes.
2. Objects must bleed off the frame.
3. Readable words only when necessary (Liveblocks-style references have too much text).
4. One accent; a whole blue card is too much.
5. Grain heavier than the references.

## Presenting concepts

Show them side by side on one canvas, named by idea ("Concept A: breakthrough card"). In chat: one line per concept on what it says, then your recommendation and the trade-off (distinctiveness vs. literalness, readability at 502px). Ask nothing else; iterate on their reaction.

## Counter-example: "What AI coding agents leave out when they build in-app social features from scratch"

The first attempt reused concept C's layout (a faded wall of code or file chips behind one big cropped card, blue detail on the card). Each concept was reasonable on its own, but next to the push-notification thumbnail they looked like the same image with new labels. The fix is not a better card; it is a different archetype — e.g. an isometric grid of feature tiles with empty slots, or a selection overlay where the agent's selection frame stops short of what matters.

## Worked example: "E-commerce personalization & the role social features play"

Ideation picked a macro detail (product card with "Liked by Ana & friends" and a blue heart) and a flow/path (friend's post → product card → blue Add to cart). The person chose the flow/path. Feedback rounds, in order — expect the same on new thumbnails and pre-empt them:
1. "Make the product clearer, not just a circle" → sneaker silhouette and price ($79 — prices in USD). (A grey Add to cart was added to the card too, then removed: it duplicated the blue button and cluttered the card.)
2. "Add social interactions" → reaction and comment chips, avatar stack, one comment preview, all grey.
3. "Center the middle card; the blue button has too much padding on the right" → focal object to the optical centre, button sized to its content.

Lessons: recognisable beats abstract when the object is the point; never repeat the blue action in grey; social traces belong on most images; balance and tight buttons are checked before showing, not after.

## Worked example: "6 Best Chat & Social Feed SDKs for Apps (2026)"

Variety check excluded wall, isometric grid, selection overlay, device crop, type-led field, macro detail and flow/path. Picks: hero icon tile (SDK package glyph with a blue "6" badge — the number is in the title — and chat, feed and `npm install` fragments at the edges) and stack (six SDK cards in depth, the front one with a Chat/Feed toggle, Chat selected in blue). At the time, no logos were used because the article wasn't provided. Rule since October 2026: with the full article, the six companies it names appear as official logos (monochrome on equal tiles — e.g. the stack or an isometric tile wall), files supplied by the person.

## Locale lesson (October 2026)

Early images used Scandinavian placeholder names (Lindqvist, Ek) and a euro price. The audience is American: use American names, USD and US conventions (`content-and-locale.md` in the master).

## Treatment lesson (October 2026)

Variants with grain only behind the foreground, and with a grainy spray-paint glow, were tried and rejected. Grain stays uniform over the whole image.
