# Template: decision

**Use for:** questions where the reader is choosing. Build vs buy, cost, timeline, "X or Y", "what should you look for in...", "should you...". Examples: "Should you build or buy social features for your app?", "How much does it cost to build in-app chat?", "Should you build an in-app community or use Discord?"

**Citations:** at least 3 external citations. You are comparing things, so cite the things: vendor documentation, pricing pages, published research, policy pages.

**Unique information:** real numbers the reader can't easily find together: cost components, effort estimates from real projects, pricing facts. Every figure is sourced.

## Typical sections

- **Answer-first block** gives the verdict and the condition that drives it: "Buy if X; build if Y."
- **What are the options?** One short, fair description of each.
- **At-a-glance table** (required for this template): rows are the dimensions a decision-maker weighs (cost, time to launch, control, maintenance, scale, data ownership, lock-in), columns are the options. Use ranges and numbers, not adjectives.
- **When is each option the right choice?** One H2 per option or one H2 with "Choose X if / Choose Y if" lines, each a single testable condition.
- **What do teams usually underestimate?** Hidden costs, migration, maintenance.

## Rules

- Apply the same criteria to every option, including social.plus. If an option beats social.plus on a dimension, say so.
- "Best X" rankings where social.plus comes first do not belong on Answer pages. Google's spam policies (updated May 15, 2026) cover attempts to manipulate generative AI responses, and reporting on the change names biased listicles. Honest vendor comparisons belong on the Blog, with social.plus identified as the publisher.
- The answer-first block and summary state the decision rule neutrally, without naming social.plus.
